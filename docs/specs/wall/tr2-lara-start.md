# Tomb Raider II: Lara and the camera at a fresh Great Wall start

Game and release: Tomb Raider II, Steam appid 225300, Windows PC `Tomb2.exe`, 912896 bytes, SHA-256 `fd9c91d14d54540b3e00c9bedafeef9e4ddb1e9542adc9f29170c7f19b1a657b`.
Reader: WallRE2. Date: 2026-11-21. Evidence: retail rules compared with `WALL.TR2` placements, sectors and animations, and with the neutral [schema 20 trace](tr2-oracle-trace.md). Scope: fresh PC campaign, normal difficulty, no saved start or cinematic Lara pose. Y increases downward; one sector is 1024 units, one click is 256, and there are 30 gameplay ticks per second. Field names below are names of the trace contract.

## Camera seed, placement and the opening view

| Moment | Rule and independent check |
|---|---|
| Before the first view | **Established.** The fresh Follow view starts with the eye at Lara's X, 1024 above her origin, and 100 toward world −Z, and the target at Lara's X/Z and the same height as the eye. Both memberships initially use her room. The starting distance is 1536, yaw and elevation requests zero, the first move uses divisor 1, and there is no bounce, subject item or selected fixed view. The initial view is calculated before the first gameplay tick, so that seed is not the tick-0 camera sample. |
| First aim height | **Established.** Target X/Z use Lara's origin. With Lara as the continuing subject, target Y moves one quarter of the remaining distance toward the point one quarter down from the top of her animated vertical bounds; the movement is truncated toward zero. In the spawn pose the top and bottom relative to her origin are −777 and 1, giving world Y 953 as the destination. The first update moves a quarter of the way there from 512, reaching 622. The nominal eye is level with the target, directly behind Lara's facing on the 1536-unit follow radius, at Z 21504. |
| Why the first eye moves to the side | **Established.** The line behind Lara stops at the last clear rear boundary, Z 22528. The next unit, Z 22527, resolves through the horizontal portal to room 78, whose floor is above the eye, so the nominal eye cannot go there. With this rear side obstructed, the camera keeps 256 units on the clear side of the failed adjacent sample, measured from Z 22527, not from the last clear point. This puts the eye at Z 22783, 257 behind the target. It recovers the remaining horizontal radius in X: the integer square root of the squared 1536 radius less the squared 257 Z separation is 1514. When the previous eye is not to the −X side of the target, the +X solution is preferred; only if its sight line is blocked does the opposite side replace it. The seeded eye and target have equal X, and the ray to (64490, 622, 22783) is clear. At this point Y 622 has at least a click of ceiling and floor clearance, including the linked ceiling, so no vertical correction is necessary. Both points resolve to room 35. These choices were checked against the rear portal sector and the clear sector on Lara's +X side. |
| Bearing and retained values at tick 0 | **Established.** The eye-to-target bearing uses the [octant and signed rounding rules](tr2-camera.md#exact-signed-bearing). Here the table index is 347, giving an angle of 1751. The direction is −X and +Z, with X larger in magnitude, so subtract a quarter-turn (16384), giving yaw −14633. At tick 0 the stored mode is Follow, smoothing divisor 10 and distance 1536. The timer, bounce, requested yaw, requested elevation and `lastFixed` are all zero; neither a current nor a previous view index or subject item is set (all four −1). |
| First gameplay tick and renewal | **Established.** Lara's ordinary floor evaluation can request a camera while she is above the floor. The spawn sector requests untimed fixed view 2, which moves immediately, using divisor 1, to its authored eye (61952, 384, 23040). The falling pose's aim height is 970; the same quarter easing from 622 gives target Y 709. The yaw is 16384. The eye's authored room is 35, but its usable viewing position resolves through the vertical link into room 78; Lara and the target remain in room 35. View 2 has no item subject, so `lastFixed` stays zero. The previous view index becomes 2 while the current view index returns to −1 and the mode returns to Follow. Both subject indices stay −1; the timer stays zero, and the yaw and elevation requests remain zero. The neighboring floor records renew this view without requiring Lara to land. Clearance can move its eye, so renewal does not guarantee identical coordinates each tick. |
| Return to Follow | **Established.** After she leaves the opening camera's sectors, the view is no longer renewed. Follow again chooses a point behind Lara's facing, corrects that destination for room geometry and eases the eye toward it with the follow divisor. It does not teleport back to the eye from tick 0. In the neutral route the previous fixed index clears at tick 77; the following samples show the eye moving as she slides, then easing after she stops. The three authored camera sectors and the [camera-renewal rules](tr2-triggers-and-interaction.md#item-masks-timers-and-action-ordering) distinguish this change from timer expiry. |

## Standing to fall, then landing

| Situation | Rule and independent check |
|---|---|
| Dry Stand finds a drop | **Established.** Once overhead or fully enclosed collision has been ruled out, compare the resolved centre-floor height to her feet at the current position. If the floor is strictly more than 384 units below, the drop takes priority over slope sliding or snapping to the floor. A gap of exactly 384 does not take this fall; wading does not use this dry drop rule. The comparison uses the live collision floor, not the initialized `roomFloor`. At the spawn, that is 2688 versus feet at 1536, a drop of 1152. |
| Entering the fall | **Established.** Enter forward fall (current and desired state 3), clip 34 at its first authored frame (492), with gravity on and descent zero. The transition adds no horizontal impulse; from idle Stand she has horizontal speed 0. Tick 1 changes the pose and gravity but leaves XYZ unchanged. From the next update add 6 to descent while below 128 and 1 once it is at least 128, then move down by the new speed. This gives descent 6 at tick 2. The opening clip advances into clip 35 while she is still airborne; neither clip propels her horizontally. |
| Contact in forward fall or forward jump (state 3) | **Established.** The tick's ordinary airborne movement and pose advance come first, then the contact tests at the resulting position. A frontal obstruction can change the pose before the landing decision (see below). Positive descent and a resolved middle floor at or above the feet, including equality, make this a landing even on a tick with frontal contact. Lara evaluates the floor action with her feet at the floor before choosing the result. A fatal impact requests Death (8), regardless of input. If she survives, Forward held without Walk chooses Run (1); otherwise she chooses Stand (2). The wading case cannot choose Run. |
| Same-tick motion after ordinary contact | **Established, inherited from the I reference.** Landing snaps Lara to the collision floor, turns gravity off and clears the incoming horizontal and vertical speeds. Her animation then advances once more. The transition ranges tested are those of the clip in force after any contact correction, at its advanced frame. The resulting clip and frame supply the grounded speed, which is applied immediately along her travel angle. The authored base speed plus acceleration times the number of frames since that clip began is in 16.16 units; discard the fractional bits by rounding down before applying the speed. There is no second airborne step. A landing clip with zero grounded speed adds no displacement, but does not undo displacement already made in the air. The held input chooses the desired state, not a numbered clip independently of the authored transitions. |
| Airborne speed before the contact step | **Established.** Once an animation advance has selected the current clip and frame, an airborne Lara keeps her prior horizontal momentum but adds the difference between that clip's authored speeds at the advanced frame and one frame earlier. Round each 16.16 speed down before taking the difference, including on the first frame of a newly entered clip: the earlier value is one frame before the new clip's start, not from the old clip. If the tick began in Fast Fall, its 95-percent damping happens before this difference. Use the resulting speed for the airborne translation; a separate grounded landing advance can follow as above. In `zip-release` tick 123 this reduces the retained 100 to 95 on entering 45/704, without Fast Fall damping yet. On tick 124, 95 first becomes 90, then 45 subtracts another 5, giving 85. |
| Authored Stand and Run in The Great Wall | **Established.** The opening fall loop 35 admits both choices at advanced frames 497–507 inclusive: Stand enters clip 82 at frame 1361; Run enters clip 92 at frame 1463. The later forward-jump loop 77 admits Stand at 1249–1275 and Run at 1250–1275, inclusive, with the same destinations. Clip 82 has zero grounded speed; clip 92 has a constant authored speed of 47 (3080192 in 16.16 units). In `stand` tick 21 the first landing therefore adds no grounded displacement; the next grounded check starts the slide. In `travel` and `movement` tick 65, the tick begins at Z 25140, the final airborne step of 50 reaches Z 25190, and clip 92 then moves another 47 along +Z, to 25237. The record ends with horizontal speed 47 and descent zero. |
| Frontal obstruction near the floor, before landing | **Established.** Ordinary forward airborne contact at the front, or at the front and top together, installs clip 31 at its first frame (460) when the middle floor is at most 128 below the feet. It sets current and desired state to the frontal reaction (14), overriding the clip's default state 2. The marked-wall catch with horizontal speed 2 is an exception. The ordinary frontal deflection moves her clear, reverses her travel angle without turning her body, and keeps one quarter of horizontal speed, truncated toward zero. Contact alone does not advance frame 460 again. If floor contact with positive descent also occurs on this tick, the normal landing rule above runs after the deflection: it can choose a new desired state and advance the already-installed clip once, not the old airborne clip. |
| Position correction for a frontal floor obstruction | **Established.** In the direction of the collision test's dominant horizontal axis, compare the centre and the front sample, taken a body radius ahead on that axis (100 units here). If the sample has crossed into the obstructing tile, push Lara back until the sample is one world unit on the clear side of the shared tile boundary. If both are in the same tile along that axis, there is no tile-boundary push on it. The transverse coordinate reverts to its pre-movement value. For motion toward lower coordinates the corrected sample is the boundary coordinate plus one; toward higher coordinates it is the boundary minus one. A combined front-and-top obstruction can instead restore the previous position. |
| `movement` tick 152: frontal obstruction and floor contact together | **Established from the contact and landing rules, checked against the level and trace.** Airborne motion leads first to (64262, 4694, 24629). The front floor at Z 24529 is 4192, a rise of 502 above the feet, exceeding the permitted 384. The middle floor is 4648, so the frontal reaction installs clip 31 frame 460. The shared tile boundary is Z 24576; the front sample moves to Z 24577, carrying Lara to Z 24677. She is still descending at contact and lands at Y 4648, with desired Stand and both speeds zero. Clip 31 has no Stand transition at the next frame and remains within its own range, so the landing advance reaches frame 461 without replacing current state 14 with the clip's default 2. Its grounded speed is zero. The floor refresh gives `roomFloor` 4684 at the corrected position, but that is not the middle floor used to land. This is not the fast-fall hard landing. |
| Grounded recovery from the frontal reaction | **Established.** While the current state is still 14, the next grounded contact uses the ordinary Stand terrain rules. If there is no blocking ceiling or unsupported drop, the steep-slope test comes before the normal floor snap. Starting a slide replaces the reaction with the first slide frame but does not snap the feet to the refreshed floor on that tick. In `movement` tick 153 the steep slope starts backward slide (state 32), clip 104 at frame 1677. Lara stays at Y 4648 while `roomFloor` stays at 4684. |
| Hard landing from fast fall (state 9) | **Established.** A forward jump can request fast fall once its descent exceeds 131; the airborne clip still has to enter that state. On each update that **starts** in Fast Fall, reduce the prior horizontal speed to 95 percent before translating the stride, with signed division toward zero (round down for a nonnegative speed). `rusty-key` keeps 47 on the tick first entering state 9, then moves 44 along +X at tick 206, from X 27742 to 27786; the next step is 41. This is separate from gravity. At floor contact in fast fall, the same impact rule decides survival. A survivor goes directly to Stand in hard-landing clip 24 at its first frame (358), regardless of Forward or Walk. Gravity and descent clear. The new clip does not advance or add a grounded step on the contact tick, leaving horizontal speed from the airborne step and contact correction. Its zero authored speed applies on the next animation advance. The [`hard-landing` fixture](tr2-oracle-trace.md#observed-routes) shows the nonfatal transition at tick 72 with horizontal speed 10 still recorded. **Established statically:** fatal fast fall requests Death instead, without installing clip 24. |
| Fall damage | **Established, mostly inherited.** The impact uses the downward speed on the landing tick, before the speed is cleared. Speeds through 140 cost nothing. From 141 through 154, lose the integer part of 1000 times the square of the excess over 140, divided by 196. At 155 or more health becomes −1 directly. Like the other II landings, remaining health of zero already selects death on that landing, unlike I's zero-health next-tick case. The first neutral landing has impact speed 120 and leaves health 1000. In [`fall-damage`](tr2-oracle-trace.md#observed-routes), descent 141 at tick 75 gains one on the next tick; impact at 142 gives 20 damage, leaving 980 HP. Ordinary terrain landing is distinct from entering water; see the [ground rules](tr2-lara-ground-and-weapons.md#ground-slopes-and-airtime). |

## Which things move first

**Established — initial updates.** The two placements with all five activation bits set are door 72 (type 107) and campfire 100 (type 253). A full startup mask is cleared and replaced by the reverse-start flag. Both have ongoing behaviour and begin active; later startup slots update first. Tick 0 therefore contains **100, then 72**, not four items. The authored masks and all published tick-zero records agree. Helicopter 101 and tiger 18 can later make a four-item list but need their own [authored wakes](tr2-great-wall-level.md#landing-and-main-route). Lara is not a third entry because her update is separate.

**Established — normal gameplay order.** After input come active world items, active effects, Lara, and the camera, in that order. The camera can already use Lara's new pose or a view nominated by her current floor action. An item awoken by Lara can appear in the sampled active list before it first updates on the following tick; the helicopter's wake during the regular Great Wall route corroborates that distinction. **Established from the retail rules, not exercised by these records:** when one active item awakens another during its own update, the new item waits for the next tick; if the current item removes itself, the item that was already due next still updates. After the camera, ambient sounds for the current room layout and the pending [flip effect](tr2-triggers-and-interaction.md#item-masks-timers-and-action-ordering) are considered, then sound finishes and the level clock advances. The sample confirms the completed clock step and updated camera; it does not establish the order of the sound/effect events by itself. The world-item, effect, Lara and camera order is inherited from [I](triggers-and-effects.md); the placements and opening view are II's.

## Authored animation links and the flare arm (Q-T2L-06)

| Situation | Retail rule and evidence |
|---|---|
| Ordinary animation advance | **Established statically.** An advance increments the current frame once, then an eligible change of state uses the matching authored source-frame range, including both endpoints. It takes the destination clip and frame as authored. If the selected frame now exceeds that clip's end, the same advance takes its end transition. That end successor's clip and frame are also taken as authored, but there is no second end test on the newly entered successor before the advance finishes. There is no bottom-of-range correction either. On the next advance the stored frame increments from the value actually entered, and the same state-change and end checks apply. This ordering applies to Lara's ordinary body animation and ordinary animated items; the separate flare arm does not use it. |
| Pose and bounds at a retained destination | **Established statically.** There is no clamp to the clip's first or last pose. An ordinary pose sample measures the stored frame from the selected clip's first authored frame, divides that elapsed count by the keyframe spacing with truncation toward zero, and selects the pose at that keyframe index using the clip's authored pose stride. The remainder determines interpolation where applicable; at an exact keyframe the pose and bounds come from that keyframe alone. A clip end does not keep that selection within the clip's intended poses. An invalid link does not imply a meaningful or readable pose in general; the following example is a specific case where the authored data can be identified. |
| What the link from 258 would do in ordinary playback | **Established by the retail advance rule and authored data; not observed in normal play.** If played through the ordinary animation advance, clip 258 at frame 109 would enter clip 254 at frame 19, even though the latter starts and ends at 0. At spacing one, the nineteenth pose step past clip 254's first pose lies on the same authored pose and bounds as clip 255's frame 19 in `WALL.TR2`. It would still be recorded as clip 254, frame 19, not clip 255. On the next advance frame 20 is outside clip 254's only state-change range (0–1) and exceeds its end, so its ordinary end successor takes it to clip 254, frame 0. |
| What Lara's held flare actually does | **Established.** Clips 254 and 258 are poses for Lara's held-flare left arm (auxiliary model, object type 9), not for a world item placed in the level. Her body keeps its own clip and bounds. The arm has a separate frame count that selects an arm pose; it does not follow the body's authored state-change or end links. During the held-flare arm motion, frames 95–109 use clip 258. At the next count the arm resets directly to frame 0 and selects clip 254, its one-frame held pose. The published [`flare-held` route](tr2-oracle-trace.md#observed-routes) confirms arm 258/109 at tick 139, then 254/0 from tick 140 through tick 165, without a body clip change at the reset. The link to frame 19 is not taken when this motion finishes in normal play. |
