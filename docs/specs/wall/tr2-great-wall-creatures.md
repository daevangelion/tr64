# Tomb Raider II: The Great Wall creatures and shared navigation
Game: Tomb Raider II, Windows PC Steam release (appid 225300).
Executable: `Tomb2.exe`, 912896 bytes; SHA-256 `fd9c91d14d54540b3e00c9bedafeef9e4ddb1e9542adc9f29170c7f19b1a657b`.
Inputs: retail executable and authored PC levels The Great Wall (`WALL.TR2`) and Temple of Xian (`Emprtomb.tr2`, giant spider comparison); I baseline on tr64 `cdfb71d`.
Reader: WallRE2. Date: 2026-11-22. Evidence: static retail behaviour and authored encounter and animation records; the schema-21 Great Wall records expose the tiger's live choices and route. Rules limited to static comparisons are marked below.

Lengths are game units, positive Y down, with 1024 per horizontal tile and 256 per click. Angles use 65,536 per full turn. Refer to [I's creature rules](lara-and-enemies.md#3-creature-ai-primer-the-rules-shared-by-every-enemy) and the [trigger and placement rules for The Great Wall](tr2-great-wall-objects.md#authored-checkpoints).

## Creature rules applying to these encounters

| Rule | II outcome and difference from I |
|---|---|
| Wake and eviction | **Established, changed.** II has five simultaneous navigation slots, not I's eight; Lara can use one for a water current. For admission, square and sum the three camera-relative differences after dividing each signed difference by 256 and rounding down. The cases below distinguish empty-pool and full-pool outcomes. |
| Target and position | **Established.** These cats, spiders, crows and dinosaurs target Lara. They use horizontal squared distance from their forward pivot to her position. Unlike I's quoted axis limit, II measures the full horizontal differences here. Ahead means less than 90° to either side (through ±16383); the separate bite-position test additionally needs a living target no more than 384 above or below (I: 256). The close tiger strikes use ahead and actual body contact, not the shared vertical bite-position test: the T. rex's bite selection is a comparison case that does require it. Later human factions can consider each other instead of Lara; do not apply that behaviour to these animals. |
| Zone and clearance | **Established, changed.** Each normal/alternate room set has four ground-zone sets instead of I's two, plus a separate flight set. A ground creature's permitted step height selects the ground zone. Its candidate boxes at awakening include those that match its initial zone in either map state, but its current navigation uses only the live state's zones. Neighbouring boxes that breach the step or drop limit are not used; doors and raised blocks can temporarily block a box. The T. rex, like I's, avoids a box that can be blocked even if it is currently open. The zone data in both PC levels and the differing small spider and crow limits corroborate the separate ground/flight cases. |
| Path following, position and heading | **Established, inherited where named.** The box search continues across updates, taking up to five expansions per tick. It searches backward from the requested destination box, considers neighbours in their authored overlap order, and admits neighbours in the search zone whose floor is within the creature's step/drop allowances in the direction of travel. A changed destination starts a new search generation and goes to the front if not already queued; it does not discard the older pending work. A box's current valid search link points toward its parent, closer to the destination. The creature turns toward a waypoint selected on this chain, then advances its animation and moves at the new clip's speed. The turn uses the **previous** move speed and turn limit; no movement speed or zero limit means no turn. Bound the signed bearing difference by that limit, halving it for a waypoint more than 90° behind and nearer than the previous speed times 16384 divided by the limit. Ground floor-following rises immediately to the linked floor and sinks toward it by at most 64 per tick, then checks room membership 512 above the grounded origin. The stored floor is the floor height even while sinking toward it. Head tracking converges by 910 angle units per tick, bounded by ±12288, not I's ±90°. |
| Destination during attack | **Established, changed.** Attacking mood can take the target's position and box every update, without I's smartness roll. For a flying creature attacking dry Lara the aim height uses her pose top. This is a desired destination: before the first search, the absence of a known destination box seeds the creature's **current** box instead, even when it has already selected Lara's box. If the chain is unfinished on a later tick, it can return a temporary waypoint rather than the desired position. These cases can draw randomness without a mood, attack or hit roll. |
| Destination while stalking | **Established, changed.** The range in each axis is 3072 plus the full width of the candidate box on that axis. Relative to the target's location, exclude the quadrant the target's facing selects. When the creature itself is in that front quadrant, exclude the opposite quadrant too. This distinguishes the candidate box's direction from the creature's current position. |
| Destination while escaping | **Established, inherited.** Use a destination whose centre is at least 5120 away on at least one horizontal axis and not on the opposite side of Lara from the creature on both axes. This is distinct from the stalking rectangle. |
| Final retirement | **Established, changed for ordinary corpses.** When an ordinary death clip ends with its deactivate command, the creature stops ticking, loses its slot and collision, and is no longer eligible for new weapon selection (health −16384). The body can still exist and a [held pistol selection](tr2-lara-arms.md#pistol-lock-and-creature-strikes) can still hit it. Carried pickups can appear where it died, not at a fixed spot determined by enemy type. A marked corpse can be cleared separately without taking back the loot. The awake pool excludes retired creatures; corpse and pickup relationships are saved separately. Small-spider disintegration is a different death. |

**Established — end links and wanted states (Q-T2K-07).** At the end of an ordinary item's clip, take the authored successor index and frame. If its state matches the current state, retain the wanted state; if it differs, use the successor's state for both current and wanted. Clear a queued state that now matches current. A state-change window is different: it takes the authored pose and current state without forcing a different wanted state, and clears the queue only if current now equals the queued state. This applies to ordinary items, not just creatures. The same-state links retain goal 8 for the tiger at `tiger-bite` 121 (292 → 293), goal 2 at `spider-bite` 11 (261 → 267), and goal 3 at `crow-bite` 59 (285 → 278). This differs from I's unconditional wanted-state reset for ordinary end links. **Established statically — Lara:** her animation advance takes the authored current state but does not reset her wanted or queued state at either kind of link, including a link to a different state. Particular locomotion or interaction decisions can still change them separately.

### Route, waypoint and random choices

**Established — search order.** A search started or changed this tick takes the requested box as destination. Insert it at the front unless it is already queued; older queued work still remains. Each expansion uses the next queued box's authored overlaps in order. The zone for this group of up to five expansions is the zone of its first box. For a proposed neighbour, compare the neighbour's floor minus the expanded box's floor to the signed drop and rise limits, including equality. An accepted clear route links back to the expanded box. Append newly queued neighbours in the order discovered. Later generations can replace older marks; at equal generation a clear route can replace a blocked one, not the reverse. A forbidden overlap flag or a blocked parent can mark a neighbour blocked, but **does not erase or replace its previously stored link**. The steering test follows the available links until it reaches the destination, reaches a box with no link, encounters a forbidden overlap, or runs out of geometrical clearance. An older usable link can still matter, as in `rex-gold` tick 75: erasing it changes both the waypoint and the gameplay random draws.

**Established — exhausted queue and retained tail.** Each expansion removes one head box. If the search checks again and finds no head, it clears the tail and stops. If the fifth expansion removes the last head, there is no sixth emptiness check: the old tail stays visible with head −1 until the next search sees the empty queue. A newly requested destination can replace the stale tail when it starts a fresh queue; a destination equal to the retained tail is still treated as already queued until that emptiness check clears it. `spider-bite` slot 10 records head −1 and tail 714 at 194, then both −1 at 195.

**Established — when the target becomes direct.** Use the creature's unchanged pre-movement origin for the box comparisons. Each box is an inclusive rectangle, ending one unit before the next tile; start with both axes unrestricted and use 512 units of edge clearance. Keep the running waypoint separate from the accumulated intersection of the boxes' transverse spans. A new box across from the origin on one axis can advance the waypoint along that open side if the origin lies in that box's transverse span: keep at least 512 inside the new near edge and narrow the accumulated transverse intersection. Only that side stays open along the resulting straight reach. A conflicting turn uses an inset edge of the accumulated intersection to make a corner, considering Z before X. On reaching the destination, take the requested coordinate on an open axis. If one axis is constrained by a straight reach, clip that running waypoint coordinate against **the destination box's** inset, not the accumulated intersection. Apply the same distinction at a temporary destination. Test a value below the low inset first, then a value above the high inset; the bounds cross by one in a one-tile-wide box. The desired Y applies when the destination is reached; an intervening corner does not go below the highest box floor traversed (1024 above it for flight).

**Established — different random positions.** Use the [gameplay stream](tr2-lara-arms.md#pistol-attempts-and-random-draws-q-t2a-01). A new box destination draws Z then X; on each axis use the box's low coordinate plus 512, plus the draw times the whole number of 32-tile groups left after removing one tile from that axis's span. Small boxes still consume both draws even when the coordinate does not vary. The Y destination is the box floor (384 above it for flight). If the chain is unfinished rather than geometrically stopped at a corner, a temporary waypoint uses the last available box. For each axis still open, draw in Z-then-X order and multiply by the box's world span less 1025, divide by 32768 and round down, then add the low coordinate plus 512. A constrained axis uses the last box's inset without a draw, except that a corner already set by a conflicting turn keeps its coordinate. The temporary Y is the last box's floor, or 384 above it for flight.

**Established — choosing a viable box.** At first admission the candidate list is sorted by level box number, including a box if it shares the creature's initial zone in either map state. A destination choice draws one number and takes the ordinal given by that draw times list size, divided by 32768 and rounded down. An eligible box is in the current live zone, not forbidden by the overlap mask and does not strictly enclose the creature's current horizontal origin (equality at a boundary is outside for this test). Before the mood and replacement choice, clear the pending request if the **creature's own current box** is marked blocked by this generation, regardless of the pending destination box's mark. `rex-gold` tick 123 distinguishes the two boxes: checking the destination instead changes the replacement and its draws. An existing non-attack request whose installed destination now fails eligibility is also discarded, and the mood is reset to Bored if the zones now agree. A blocked enemy box or blocked route from the creature's box is treated as an enemy in a different zone.

| Time in the decision | Established ordering and costs |
|---|---|
| Mood change, before selecting a destination | Apply the mood comparison below. When leaving Attack, draw Z and X for a point in the old installed destination box before clearing the pending request. That point can remain as a fallback even if no replacement is chosen. Other mood changes clear the request without these two draws. |
| Attack | Ask for the preceding enemy position and box with no position or selection draw. For flight against dry Lara use her nearest pose's top bound for the destination height. |
| Bored | Draw one candidate every update. If viable and suitable for stalking a living enemy, pick its point with two more draws and become Stalking. Otherwise pick that viable box with two draws only if there is no pending destination. Do not try a second candidate after rejection. |
| Stalking | Keep a pending destination still suitable for stalking without re-drawing. Otherwise draw one candidate. A viable stalking box takes the usual two position draws. If the viable box does not suit stalking but there was no pending choice, take its point anyway and revert to Bored if the zones differ. |
| Escape | Draw one candidate even if keeping a pending destination. A viable candidate can be chosen as an escape only when no request is pending; if it fails the escape test but qualifies for stalking in the same zone, take its point and become Stalking. Both successful new destinations take the usual two draws. |
| Initial seeding and steering | If no box has ever been installed, the first search is seeded in the creature's current box with two position draws, even if Attack just requested Lara's position. Resolve the corridor now, with temporary draws only as above. A species' pose choice (such as the tiger's roar) comes afterwards. |

**Established — stalking and escape bearings.** For the stalking ranges in the table above, compare centres to the target on each horizontal axis, including equality at the distance limit. In clockwise order the box-centre quadrants are nonpositive X/nonpositive Z, nonpositive X/positive Z, positive X/positive Z, and positive X/nonpositive Z. The target's forward quadrant is its signed yaw divided by 16384 rounding down, plus two. The creature's own quadrant uses its X/Z relative to the target. Escape excludes a box if it is on the opposite side of the target from the creature in both axes, treating zero coordinate difference as nonpositive.

### Collision after the creature turns

**Established — terrain checks.** Advance the clip and take its proposed stride after the normal turn. Check boxes at the advanced pose's top Y, starting from the origin's linked room; the sample at the old horizontal position supplies the starting room for the one at the new position. If the proposed box is absent, in a different zone, or beyond the step/drop limits, compare the **proposed X tile** to each previous tile index. The X correction compares it to old X; unexpectedly, the Z correction compares that **same proposed X tile** to old Z, not to the proposed Z tile. Tile indices are world coordinates divided by 1024, rounded down. For either comparison, a smaller index snaps the affected axis to the low edge of its old tile, a larger one to its inclusive high edge, and equality leaves its proposed coordinate alone. Then resolve the box again. This can snap Z even without crossing a Z boundary: at `tiger-death` 271 the proposed point (65550, 35290) fails the zone check; X becomes 65535 and Z becomes 35839. This is the recorded cross-axis quirk, not a two-axis rollback of rejected movement.

**Established — neighbouring radius check.** At the resulting point, check each tile edge strictly closer than the creature's wall radius. Test the point one radius farther out along that axis. A missing or forbidden box, an excessive step or drop, or a drop steeper than the positive step allowance onto a floor lower than the next route box's floor rejects the point. Unlike the stride-origin check, a difference in zone alone does **not** reject a neighbouring radius sample. `tiger-death` tick 248 keeps the step at (65201, 5805, 35414), rather than pushing the tiger away from the neighbouring zone. If there is no next link from the proposed box, compare with its own floor. Flight also refuses a box when the pose-top sample is below its floor plus flight speed. A rejected side keeps a full wall radius of space: relative to the tile origin, the limits are radius on the low side and 1024 minus radius on the high side.

**Established statically — diagonals and other items.** Where both axis probes fit but a diagonal probe one radius beyond each axis fails, correct one axis:

| Corner by tile sides | Correct if yaw is in the inclusive band | Correct otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| Low X, low Z | Z at −24575…8191 | X |
| High X, low Z | Z at −8191…24575 | X |
| Low X, high Z | X at −8191…24575 | Z |
| High X, high Z | X at −24575…8191 | Z |

Any nonzero radius correction repeats the same signed body turn once after the displacement; it does not take a second stride. A peer-object obstruction instead rejects the moved position if another moving, active item preceding the creature in its room's item order lies at a 3-D squared distance strictly less than the creature's radius squared. Lara is excluded from that check. For a grounded animal the final floor snaps up or follows downward by up to 64 and the room is resolved 512 above the new height, as above.

**Established statically — contact pose and timing.** Lara's own collision measures contact after the creature moved and after Lara advanced her body animation and proposed movement, but **before this creature pushes her** and before her ordinary floor/wall collision. An earlier item's push can already have moved her. Use both bodies' nearest authored keyframes, with the [effective shortened interval](tr2-lara-slide-height-and-gaze.md#pose-sampling-at-a-clips-tail-q-t2c-07). Lara's meshes use the authored pose and her whole-body rotation; **no** live torso, head or arm controls, rendered hit pose or weapon overlays affect her contact spheres. The vertical bounds must strictly overlap and Lara's centre must lie inside the creature's yaw-relative horizontal bounds expanded by her radius. Then compare authored mesh spheres in world space. Require positive radii and squared centre distance strictly less than the squared sum of radii. Each overlapping Lara sphere marks the bit of the associated creature mesh. This is a geometry test, without a gameplay roll. Creatures can use these bits before advancing their attack pose on the **next** tick.

**Established — exact contact arithmetic (Q-T2K-09).** For the preliminary test, use the vertical extents plus each body's world Y, with no pitch/roll applied to those extents; the two intervals must overlap strictly. Turn the horizontal offset of Lara **from the creature's origin** into the creature's local frame by inverse yaw only, summing each pair of signed products before rounding down at the 14-bit scale. Equality at an expanded face qualifies. For the spheres, begin at each body's world origin with its full body yaw, then local pitch, then local roll. Apply the root's authored translation and nearest key rotation, then the remaining bones' translations and nearest key rotations in parent order. At each joint use the authored rotation order yaw, pitch, roll, and then any enabled extra Y, X, Z rotations. A positive yaw takes forward toward +X, positive pitch takes forward toward −Y, and positive roll takes +X toward +Y. Use II's [quarter-wave sine and cosine](tr2-lara-ground-and-weapons.md#projecting-speed-along-a-heading-q-t2c-09) for every rotation, including live and body angles. Sum the signed products for each matrix entry before rounding down at the 14-bit scale, separately after each axis rotation. Translations, including the sphere's authored local mesh centre, accumulate at that scale; round down each final centre coordinate once before adding the body's world origin. Radii are the unchanged authored world-unit radii. The strict squared overlap test above uses those rounded centres, not intermediate transforms.

**Established — inclusive faces in playback.** In `tiger-bite` 163, tiger nearest frame 64 of 289 gives forward maximum 511. Lara's local forward coordinate is 611, exactly on the 100-widened face: the sphere check still runs. For example tiger mesh 22 overlaps Lara mesh 4 (radii 151 and 87, squared centre distance 23701). Bits 22, 25 and 26 give 104857600. In `tiger-bite` 155, her local forward coordinate 567 is already exactly the nearest unexpanded push face. It is a valid contact: her position does not change, but the enabled bump advances from hit frame 1 to 2 in quadrant 3. A nonzero push distance is not required. Contact outside the unexpanded push rectangle may still produce sphere bits without a push or bump.

**Established — live joint mapping.** Starting with mesh 0 as root, each following mesh uses the corresponding authored bone in pose order, with its translations and the parent changes in the bone data. Add live joint rotations after the authored local rotation, before placing the sphere; the transform also carries to descendants until their authored parent changes. For the tiger, add the first `jointYaw` to **mesh 22's** joint, around local Y. The second angle is not used by this tiger. The transform differs from the blood origin on joint 26. On `tiger-bite` tick 121, the authored spheres and live rotation reproduce the overlap of tiger mesh 22 with Lara mesh 4 and the resulting bit 22. With that rotation omitted the spheres do not overlap. The recorded Lara origin is sufficient for this comparison because there is no push on that tick.

**Established — body push and hit reaction are not bite damage.** Ordinary contact can push dry or wading Lara without taking HP; regular surface and submerged movement disable the push. In the creature's nearest-pose horizontal bounds, **without** the preliminary radius enlargement, move Lara to the nearest face (ties: low X, high X, high Z, low Z). Keep Y. Test in the direction from Lara's saved starting position with the ordinary 762 body, open drop, 384 rise and zero ceiling threshold. Reject a blocked push to the saved start; otherwise keep the new origin as the reference for remaining contacts. The hit reaction, if enabled and the creature's bounds are more than 256 tall, is registered at push time, before the room test can reject the push. Compute its quadrant from Lara's yaw minus the bearing from the rotated creature-bounds midpoint to Lara just before the push, plus 40960, wrapping the result as unsigned before taking quarters of a turn. Each qualifying reaction increments `hitFrame`, capped at 34, and plays sound 31 if the old frame was zero. At the start of contact evaluation reset the quadrant to −1; if no reaction supplies another quadrant by the end, reset `hitFrame` to zero. `tiger-bite` first shows quadrant 3, frame 1 at tick 141, then frame 2 at 142, without a new bite or nonzero `hitTimer`.

**Established statically — timed reaction is separate.** Ordinary creature push neither sets nor decrements `hitTimer`. A hazard can separately set it and supply its source; for example the flying knife hit sets 5. After processing item contacts, if a timed reaction is pending, use the bearing from Lara to its source with the same quadrant formula, increment and cap `hitFrame` the same way, and decrement `hitTimer` by one. It can replace the contact quadrant in the same tick. Lara's contact pass can also clear `wasHit` after a bite that already changed HP. No timed reaction is exercised by the recorded tiger push.

### Admission outcomes

| Slot and distance conditions | Result |
|---|---|
| **Established:** A vacant slot | Use the first vacant slot. Both ordinary and forced admission can use it. |
| **Established:** Full pool, ordinary request | Among occupants farther from the camera than the entrant, evict the most distant. An occupant tied with the entrant is not eligible; among equally distant eligible victims, the first one in slot order wins. |
| **Established:** Full pool, forced request | Treat the entrant's distance as zero: any occupant with positive scaled distance can be considered. The most distant still wins and the first such slot wins a tie. |
| **Established:** Full pool, nobody eligible | No slot is assigned or evicted. A creature still hidden can try again later. A forced request isn't proof that any creature was evicted; in particular, a full pool with all distances zero has no eligible victim. |

### Mood transitions

**Established — inherited mood priorities.** A missing or non-living target gives bored before these comparisons. The cases contrast the first hit response, zone changes without a hit, and continued escape.

| Mood at start of the choice | Violent | Nonviolent |
|---|---|---|
| **Established:** Bored or stalking | Same zone → attack; hit in a different zone → escape; otherwise no change from this choice. | Hit across zones or hit with a 2048/32768 fear roll → escape first. Otherwise in the same zone choose attack within 3072, or when stalking without a pending box; otherwise stalk. Different zones without the hit response do not start pursuit. |
| **Established:** Attacking | Across zones → bored, otherwise keep attacking. | Hit across zones or hit with a 2048/32768 fear roll → escape first. Otherwise across zones → bored; same zone → keep attacking. |
| **Established:** Escaping | Same zone → attack; otherwise keep escaping. | Same zone → stalk with chance 256/32768; otherwise escape remains. |

**Established statically — nonviolent draw cost.** On a recent hit the nonviolent Bored, Stalking and Attack choices take the fear draw even when the enemy is across zones (where escape happens regardless of the draw). Nonviolent Escape takes its 256/32768 recovery draw only in the same zone. The tiger uses the violent rule; neither zone change nor tiger hit alone calls for a mood draw.
