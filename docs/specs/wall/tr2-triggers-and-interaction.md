# Tomb Raider II: floor triggers and item interactions
Game: Tomb Raider II, Windows PC Steam release (appid 225300).
Executable: `Tomb2.exe`, 912896 bytes; SHA-256 `fd9c91d14d54540b3e00c9bedafeef9e4ddb1e9542adc9f29170c7f19b1a657b`.
Inputs: retail executable and authored PC levels The Great Wall (`WALL.TR2`), Venice (`BOAT.TR2`), and Offshore Rig (`RIG.TR2`, single-use control comparison only). I baseline: the existing wall specifications on tr64 `cdfb71d`.
Reader: WallRE2. Date: 2026-11-21. Evidence: static retail rules, cross-checked as indicated; not a runtime playthrough.

Distances are game units, Y increases downward, and durations use the 30 Hz logic tick. Angles in the reach tables use 65,536 units per turn. Terms follow [the glossary](glossary.md). The comparison is with [I's triggers](triggers-and-effects.md) and [I's item interactions](objects-missing.md#25-pickups-keys-puzzles).

## Floor evaluation

**Established — inherited.** Each tick advances active items, active effects, Lara, the camera and the current flip effect, in that order. Lara evaluates her sector on land, at the surface and submerged; landing can also evaluate it at the new height, so a pad can respond to the landing itself. Within a sector evaluation, fixed-view renewal occurs **before** testing whether the trigger qualifies; it is skipped during a heavy view. The within-sector ordering is evident in the contrast between a dummy that can maintain a view and a key or switch that cannot operate its targets until qualified.

| Source or class | II rule and comparison with I |
|---|---|
| Ordinary (0), pad (1), antipad (6), combat (7), dummy (8) | **Established, inherited.** Ordinary can fire in the air; pad and antipad need the origin exactly at the recorded floor. Combat responds with guns ready, not while drawing or putting them away. A dummy can keep a fixed view but does not execute the list. The checks contrast ordinary with grounded types, ready weapons with the two transitions, and dummy camera refresh with its inert actions. |
| Switch (2), key (3), pickup (4) | **Established.** As in I, the additional controlling-item word identifies what must be used or taken before the actions can apply. The Great Wall and Venice contain switch and key records; The Great Wall also contains a pickup record. Their distinct results are described below. |
| Heavy (5) | **Established.** As in I, only weight-driven evaluation runs these actions, and weight-driven evaluation does not run another class's actions. A moving boulder and a settled pushable block can still operate a heavy trigger. The first mine blast can also operate one. A ridden speedboat additionally tests the sector as Lara, before testing it as weight; the empty hull does neither. Cross-check: the boulder remains eligible during motion, the block upon settling, and the boat only while ridden. |
| Antitrigger (9) | **Established, new.** It can undo its targets while Lara is above the floor, where an antipad would not respond. Both clear the named item mask bits. Venice's authored lists contain antitriggers distinct from its ordinary activations. |
| Deadly floor (floor function 5, before a trigger) | **Established, changed.** Lara on her recorded floor can burn as in I; a water status other than dry also permits the burn check when her feet are above the floor. In either case the recorded floor must match the real floor found far below and her health must not already be negative. Heavy evaluation skips the burn but can still honor a following entry. The real-floor requirement rules out burning when a different surface is carrying Lara. |
| Climbable face (floor function 6, before a trigger) | **Established, new.** A marked face can be climbable without a following trigger; an ordinary evaluation without a matching face clears its availability. Both checked levels contain standalone face marks as well as sectors with gameplay triggers. |

**Established — inherited.** A closed trapdoor can support Lara or block her from below without being activated. When a sector lists an object, its surface may contribute to the sector's floor or ceiling; the controlling item of a switch, key or pickup record can contribute a surface too if it has one. Whether the listed object receives an activation is a separate question. The distinction follows from comparing solid support and underside clearance with the conditions for firing the same sector's actions.

## Item masks, timers and action ordering

**Established — inherited.** A one-shot locked item ignores later item actions, including anti actions. A switch toggles the five-bit mask positions it names, a positive trigger adds them, and an antipad clears them. The timer refreshes even when the mask is incomplete: 0 is untimed, 1 remains one tick, and larger values are seconds. Only a complete mask permits ordinary activation and an ordinary trigger's one-shot lock. The timer counts down on consultation only while the mask is complete. A normal item is still triggered on the final counted tick; an incomplete mask or an expired timer instead triggers a reverse item. These outcomes agree with I; the switch's timed return and an item placed with a full, reverse-start mask distinguish an elapsed timer from an incomplete mask.

**Established — changed.** A one-shot antipad or antitrigger locks the target even if its mask is incomplete afterward. A normal positive action with an incomplete result cannot lock it, and neither can change an existing lock. Comparing the incomplete-mask cases shows that locking the mask is not itself an activation.

**Established.** Completing a visible dormant creature's mask requests forced admission: it may displace an eligible occupant, not a creature at zero scaled camera distance. A hidden creature can wait invisibly if it is not admitted. The [five-slot rules](tr2-great-wall-creatures.md#creature-rules-applying-to-these-encounters) distinguish these cases; a non-creature without per-tick behaviour never enters that set. The inherited distinction is between forced and ordinary admission, not an unconditional right to evict.

| Item situation | Established result and I comparison |
|---|---|
| An anti action leaves the mask incomplete | **Established, inherited.** It does not stop the item's ticks or force any animation. An already ticking ordinary target reads as untriggered on its next check; an already ticking reverse target reads as triggered. The converse holds on a complete mask with a live timer. These opposing responses follow from the same mask change in the two target types. |
| Anti action names no mask bits | **Established.** No bit is cleared; the timer still refreshes and a one-shot anti action can still lock the resulting mask. This distinguishes a zero mask from clearing all five bits. The lock on an incomplete result is the II difference described above. |
| Item placed with a full mask | **Established, inherited.** It starts with the reverse mark and no activation bits; it starts ticking immediately if it has per-tick behaviour, rather than waiting for a trigger to fire. An inert placed object still cannot tick. This was checked separately from ordinary completion of a partial mask after level start. |
| Newly activated versus already ticking item | **Established, inherited.** A newly activated non-creature clears old touch and takes active status, including one previously hidden. A no-behaviour target still does not enter the ticking set. A creature that becomes eligible to wake clears its old touch too; a hidden creature without a free or evictable slot can remain hidden. An already ticking item whose mask or timer changes does not clear touch just for that change. On the same completed mask, the distinction is whether the item was already ticking. |

**Established — ordering.** The actions take effect in list order except for the final fixed or heavy camera subject, the requested room swap and the chosen effect. The camera subject is selected for that view first, then the rooms swap once, then the chosen effect starts if the list permits it. In particular, an effect can first run after the camera has been updated for that tick; it is not an immediate mid-list event. This ordering was checked against the flip/no-flip effect cases and against the later camera and effect updates in the same gameplay tick.

| Action | II rule and cross-check |
|---|---|
| Fixed camera (1) and look target (6) | **Established.** As in I, a new fixed view will not interrupt Look or combat, and neither a combat trigger nor a timed switch release arms it. If the camera number is the same as last frame, a non-switch cannot arm a new countdown, but can renew the running view; a switch is exempt from the same-camera bar. The camera has its own one-shot lock and smoothing of eight times its speed code plus one. Unlike I's one-tick exception, a camera timer of 1 means 30 ticks. The final eligible target becomes the fixed or heavy subject. The comparison separates renewing a running view from arming it anew under the same camera number. |
| Camera termination | **Established, two cases.** An actual trigger or object-surface lookup takes the end-of-list bit from the camera's extra word, as in I. Renewing a fixed view uses the end bit of the camera action word while still stepping past the extra word. In both checked PC levels, terminal camera records have the end bit only in the extra word. Thus those records end the actions and surfaces without necessarily ending camera renewal at that same word. |
| Sink/current (2) | **Established, changed setup.** A current request specifies a target position and box and a pull strength six times the authored signed strength. A new position in the same box replaces the old target position; [I retains the old one](triggers-and-effects.md#7-sink-code-2-underwater-current). Renewal of the request is distinct from [the physical displacement in water](tr2-water-and-flares.md#currents). |
| Flip map/force on/force off (3–5), effect (9) | **Established.** As in I, switches toggle flip-slot bits; other types accumulate them, a completed slot can lock one-shot, and force-on or force-off needs a complete slot. In II an effect with no flip-related action in its list starts without a swap. With any flip-related action in the list, the world must actually swap for the effect to start. A locked slot can therefore suppress the effect. The last qualifying effect begins at age zero after the swap decision. These distinctions come from contrasting an effect alone, an effect with an unsuccessful flip request, and an effect with a successful one; Venice has authored effect and flip records. |
| Finish level (7) | **Established, inherited.** An authored exit marks the level complete when its action occurs. Completion takes effect before another gameplay tick, not partway through the current list. The checked levels both have authored finish actions. |
| Soundtrack (8) | **Established, changed.** Tracks 2–63 qualify, whereas 1 and 64 do not. Outside a switch, overlapping remembered bits suppress a track; a one-shot remembers the supplied bits. A partial mask does not stop a song or require the other bits before play. An untimed new request plays immediately; a timed one holds a countdown equal to the low eight bits of timer times 30, and accepted re-evaluation plays it on the tick bringing a nonzero count to zero. These cases contrast range boundaries, timed versus untimed, and an overlapping versus disjoint mask; both levels contain authored track actions. |
| Old floor-secret action (10), clear bodies (11) | **Established, changed.** Unlike I, action 10 does not collect a secret and does not prevent a valid action later in the list. A dragon pickup does collect one. Action 11 clears marked corpses, not every dead creature or its released cargo. A placed creature's high item-flag bit (15) marks it for cleanup; it becomes clearable on death and can remain so on load. A marked body and an unmarked body respond differently to the same clear request. |

**Established — camera renewal details.** As in I, renewal doesn't wait for a switch to settle or a key to be used. The controlling-item word comes before any camera or target word and is treated like an ordinary item number there; it doesn't renew a view. A camera matching the previous number can renew a fixed view while its timer has not expired and Look or combat is not in force. A matching camera with either obstruction instead has its timer marked expired. A different camera number does not renew that fixed view. Where several camera choices are encountered, the final one's match or mismatch determines whether to keep the current subject in the renewal; a later mismatch can clear the subject even after a prior match renewed the view. A remembered look subject without a camera can continue if it has not already been looked at, or if it is the same one that was just used. With no current camera number, a still-positive fixed countdown expires. A heavy view already in force doesn't renew this way. Cross-check: the outcomes for no camera, a matching camera and an unmatched camera differ; early Great Wall camera records that end only in their extra word are each followed by a terminal word that contributes neither camera nor subject. This is a confirmed example, not a promise about arbitrary neighbouring floor data.

**Established — soundtrack edge cases, changed from I.** A nonzero authored timer can wrap to an initial zero count (for example 128 seconds); that pending request never reaches playback merely from being revisited, because it has no positive count to expire. A different accepted timed track takes its place as the sole pending request. With playback enabled, a different accepted untimed track replaces it immediately. Revisiting the same pending track counts its existing count down, rather than starting over. A timed one-shot with nonoverlapping remembered bits can set new bits on its first evaluation; if the next evaluation overlaps them, it is suppressed before advancing the timer. Thus a one-shot with nonzero mask need not finish counting down by itself. These results contrast no count with one remaining tick, selection of the same track with a different track, and switch evaluations with ordinary mask suppression.

## Reach and commitment

**Established — reach bounds.** The position and angle ranges below use the item's local interaction pose. Exact equality at a lower or upper edge qualifies; a value outside the edge does not. This was checked at both ends of position and angle comparisons. Reaching an item does not in itself acquire or use it.

**Established — shared pickup frame.** Each ordinary item encountered within reach is checked separately at the commit frame. Acquiring one item does not end the contact pass, change Lara's pickup pose, or consume a one-item permission; a later eligible item in the same pass can be acquired on that same frame, without Action still held. `shotgun-ammo` tick 43 takes the co-located box 29 and large medikit 28 together: shotgun reserve 12 to 24, ring type 172 from one to two. **Established — ordinary removal inherited.** An ordinary pickup goes invisible and out of that room's visible objects but remains an identifiable placed item, unlike the world flare which is removed. This distinction was checked against the once-only trigger response and the two different pickup states that can be loaded. The disappearance and retained identity agree with I, unlike the absent ordinary pickup tally noted above.

**Established — pickup trigger, inherited.** A pickup trigger waits for its ordinary pickup to disappear and responds once when next evaluated. Lara can still have her hands occupied. The Great Wall's authored type 4 record names dragon object 190 and wakes a Tyrannosaurus (214), not the pool tiger or either key.

### Medipacks from the main ring (Q-CARTUI-01)

**Established statically — eligibility and side effects.** Confirming a carried medipack heals only a living Lara with health strictly between 0 and 1000. Selecting the item with health at least 1000 or at most 0 neither consumes it nor increases the use statistic. There is no separate use-in-progress state: healing happens once at confirmation, before gameplay resumes. There is no medicine-specific check on her hands, weapon, water mode or pose once the main ring is available. The medipack does not alter air, burn or any other gameplay status; it is not a poison cure. A successful use plays the common medipack sound. The fixture checks two successful uses, not the refusals, sound or other Lara modes.

| Medicine and main-ring type | Established: health restored, capped at 1000 | Established: saved `mediUsed` credited |
|---|---:|---:|
| Small, 171 | 500 | 1 |
| Large, 172 | 1000 | 2 |

**Established — quantity and cursor.** One pack is taken on success. A remaining quantity stays in the same entry; when the final pack is taken, the entry disappears and later entries close the gap. The main-ring selection still records the confirmed item's zero-based position, even when it is now past the last entry. The next opening of the main ring starts at position 0 regardless of the former selection; the key-ring selection is not changed by the medicine. Removal from a stack greater than one and the unconditional reset on opening are established statically. The two one-pack removals, the saved positions 5 and 4, and the second menu beginning at 0 are checked in the native fixture.

**Established — native fixture, not a fixed menu cost.** `medipacks` takes blade damage before opening the ring. The small restores 300 to 800 on the first resumed tick, 169, and the large restores 800 to 1000 on tick 191. `mediUsed` goes from 0 to 1 to 3; the two removals consume only the starting medipacks. World movement and the normal gameplay update pause while the menu is open. The small selection adds 87 saved ticks and the large 81, in addition to their resumed gameplay updates. Across those whole sample intervals the presentation stream advances 88 and 82 times, respectively; the gameplay stream does not advance. Menu duration depends on inputs and cadence, not on the medicine's size. See [the manifest and capture contract](tr2-oracle-trace.md#repeatable-fixtures).

### Keys and puzzle sockets

**Established — reach and matching.** Lara presses Action to select from the key ring or uses an already selected item. The reach is X −200…200, Y 0, Z 312…512 and angles ±1820/±5460/±1820, from a grounded stand (state 2) with free hands. A keyhole accepts only its matching key; a puzzle socket accepts only its matching puzzle item. The Rusty Key record checks an out-of-reach Action followed by in-reach use with one carried key.

**Established statically — refusal and multiple keys.** Outside reach, Action does not open the key ring for this lock. Inside reach with several keys, the choice is made in the native ring, not found automatically by matching the lock. Confirming a wrong key consumes nothing, leaves the lock unchanged and can sound refusal 2. A used lock does not reopen the choice or consume a second key; an in-reach Action there can also sound refusal 2. No published fixture exercises an in-reach mismatch, a retry on a spent socket, or choosing among several keys.

### Levers and button switches

**Established — dry entry.** Action from standing with free hands, no gravity and a ready switch. Reach is X −220…220, Y 0, Z 292…512 and angles ±1820/±5460/±1820. Lara turns to face exactly as the switch does, starts her use motion, and cannot use her hands for another task while it plays. The lever clips and placements in Venice and the plain lever in The Great Wall corroborate the three ordinary dry variants below. The special press is distinguished by one-use behaviour, not inferred from the appearance of a Venice button.

**Established — same-tick use, later switch mask (Q-W-04).** An accepted dry interaction starts Lara's use pose, puts the switch in active status, and applies one ordinary switch animation advance immediately. This local mask is not the activation mask issued to the linked target upon settlement.

A loaded state-1 button whose action already fired does not fire again; one saved in state 1 without that mark is still eligible on evaluation unless already deactivated. This concerns the control's saved state, not whether every possible mid-press Lara pose can be saved. Venice's type 103 instead uses Lara's normal clip 197 and can be pressed again when its own motion settles.

**Established — bounds and snaps by variant.** The one-shot and repeatable dry controls share the same reach but use different final offsets; even the ordinary wall lever can take a valid reach without translating Lara. The submerged control uses neither the dry bounds nor any instant snap. The I wall trigger specification establishes lever settlement and timed return but does not give an interaction box or snap for its switch, so numeric I switch parity cannot be inferred from the retained timer rule.

**Established — submerged lever, object 105.** Press Action while in submerged tread (13) with hands free. Reach is X/Y ±1024, Z −1024…512 and angles ±14560 on all three axes. Lara approaches (0, 0, 108) with the same 16-unit position and 364-unit angle allowance as underwater pickup. The operation does not start while she is approaching. On alignment, with the lever in state 0 or 1, she stops moving through the water, occupies her hands in state 40 and moves the lever to its other position before returning to tread. Venice has this lever and its two settle animations; the underwater reach is not the dry snap.

**Established — settled switches, inherited except as noted.** The actions take effect once the switch reaches its authored deactivate command, not at the sound or Lara's first interaction frame. An untimed lever stays where it settled and is available again. A lever timed in state 0 stays moving-capable for the given seconds or for one tick with value 1, then requests a return to state 1. If that timed return is evaluated at settlement it toggles the target mask back but does not re-arm the camera. The Great Wall example has no such lever timer, so its second manual press can re-arm the view. The two settled states and separate camera timeout distinguish these operations. The type 92 button differs from the ordinary I lever because it can fire before settlement and only once.
