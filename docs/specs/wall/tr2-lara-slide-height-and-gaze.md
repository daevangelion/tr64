# Tomb Raider II: slide jumps, sector heights, and Lara’s gaze

Game and release: Tomb Raider II, Steam appid 225300, Windows PC `Tomb2.exe`, 912896 bytes, SHA-256 `fd9c91d14d54540b3e00c9bedafeef9e4ddb1e9542adc9f29170c7f19b1a657b`.
Reader: WallRE2. Date: 2026-11-21. Evidence: retail behaviour checked against `WALL.TR2`, the published PC oracle routes in the [trace contract](tr2-oracle-trace.md), and the licensed wall baseline for Tomb Raider I. Y increases downward; all angles are signed turn units, 65536 to a full turn. Durations are in gameplay ticks.

## Jumping out of a slide (Q-T2L-02)

| Sliding direction | Established conditions and authored transition |
|---|---|
| Forward, state 24 | Jump held without Back requests forward jump (state 3). Clip 70 admits that state at authored frames 1133–1154 inclusive, entering clip 76 at frame 1233. Back held blocks a new jump request, even with Jump held. |
| Backward, state 32 | Jump held without Forward requests back jump (state 25). Clip 104 admits it at frames 1677–1686 inclusive, and its loop 105 at 1686–1709 inclusive. Both enter clip 74 at frame 1189. Forward held blocks a new jump request, even with Jump held. |

**Established — timing and comparison.** The input is held, not a new-press test, and has no extra frame requirement; the authored transition tests the frame after the current animation advances. A blocked input does not cancel an existing animation goal on its own. The animation still needs to remain eligible: for example automatic slope-end and edge-fall rules are separate from a jump request. I requests the jump on Jump alone in both slide states; the opposing-direction vetoes are II's change. In `movement` at tick 95, Forward is held with Jump, so Lara remains in the backward slide and clip 104 advances from frame 1680 to 1681. This is not a closed animation window: frame 1681 lies inside the authored transition range.

**Established — positive playback.** The [forward `slide-jump` and `slide-jump-backward` fixtures](tr2-oracle-trace.md#observed-routes) confirm the permitted transitions. At forward tick 23, Jump without Back changes slide clip 70 to 76 at frame 1233. At backward tick 92, Jump without Forward changes slide clip 104 to 74 at frame 1189. Both are distinct from the blocked `movement` attempt.

**Established — travel versus facing.** The slide's terrain contact uses the current body yaw for a forward slide and the body yaw plus a half-turn for a backward slide (wrap as a signed turn). On side contact Lara is shifted clear and her body yaw deflects by 910 away from the contact. That deflection does **not** turn the movement angle already selected for the same contact. If she remains in the same slide on the same slope, the next movement still uses that retained angle until the next slide contact takes its direction from the then-current body yaw. If a new slope or a different sliding direction is selected, the new slide instead sets travel to downhill; the backward pose faces uphill. Compare the separate side corrections with the slope-start cases, and the backward-slide samples with yaw −32768 and travel angle 0. The half-turn relation is inherited from I.

## Sector heights and room boundaries (Q-T2L-03)

**Established — encoded slopes in retail II.** Start with the signed height click of the relevant sector multiplied by 256. In the floor or ceiling slope word, the low byte is the signed world X coefficient and the high byte the signed world Z coefficient, each sign-extended independently. One axis's in-sector offset is the least nonnegative remainder of the world coordinate modulo 1024, in 0–1023 inclusive; the opposite-edge distance is 1023 minus that remainder. The room origins are tile-aligned, so either origin gives the same remainder inside valid room data. Selecting a boundary sector does not clamp these offsets.

**Established — integer rounding in retail II.** Multiply each signed coefficient by its nonnegative distance, then divide each product by four and round toward negative infinity. A negative product stays negative during rounding; for example, −9 divided by four rounds to −3. A positive product stays positive; 9 divided by four rounds to 2. Only then add or subtract that rounded quantity as the table specifies. In particular, a nonnegative floor slope adds its rounded positive product; it does not subtract a product made negative before rounding. Round each axis separately, then combine both contributions with the base height.

| Surface and axis | Established contribution for a negative coefficient | Established contribution for a nonnegative coefficient |
|---|---|---|
| Floor, X or Z | Subtract the rounded product of coefficient and offset. | Add the rounded product of coefficient and opposite-edge distance. |
| Ceiling, X | Add the rounded product of coefficient and opposite-edge distance. | Subtract the rounded product of coefficient and offset. |
| Ceiling, Z | Add the rounded product of coefficient and offset. | Subtract the rounded product of coefficient and opposite-edge distance. |

**Established — no ordinary height clamp.** Ordinary slope arithmetic does not clamp the sum to a sector boundary or a click. A camera query that asks to treat steep slopes as flat ignores the entire slope if either coefficient has magnitude at least 3; it does not cap the coefficients. That is not the ordinary Lara room-floor measurement. The campaign can also specify a floor base to override: a matching base is treated as 16384 before tilt, but The Great Wall does not set this override. Solid surfaces supplied by an object, such as a trapdoor, are independent of the sector's authored tilt. The separate camera steep-slope choice and the ordinary floor read corroborate why a camera result need not agree with Lara's.

| Lookup step | Established rule |
|---|---|
| Initial room and boundary sector | Start with the current room. Subtract its X/Z origin and round each quotient by 1024 down to select the tile; a coordinate exactly on an internal edge belongs to the next tile toward the positive axis. At or outside the first or last Z row, select that end row and constrain X to the interior columns (column 1 through the number of columns minus 2). At interior Z rows, constrain X to the whole column range, including either end. This is a sector choice within the current room, not a global search for a room containing the point. |
| Horizontal portals, then room membership | If the selected sector links horizontally, repeat the same selection at the original world XYZ in the linked room until there is no horizontal link. Then use the raw click heights, not sloped floor or ceiling heights, to decide vertical membership. Y at or below the raw floor follows a downward link, including equality; otherwise Y strictly above the raw ceiling follows an upward link. Equality with a ceiling does not cross upward. Continue in the chosen vertical direction until the next boundary is not crossed or there is no link. In linked vertical rooms select the sector directly at the original X/Z relative to that room's origin; do not apply horizontal-edge clamping or horizontal links again in this vertical descent/ascent. |
| Height queries after room membership | A floor measurement reaches the bottom of the downward-link chain from the resolved sector regardless of the sample Y; a ceiling measurement reaches the top of the upward chain. Each uses the original X/Z in its final sector. Overhead object surfaces are considered separately from the floor-side linked sectors. Thus the surface measured, room membership, and raw click used to cross into a linked room are three different facts. |
| Lara's dry stored floor | Ordinary dry movement tests collision with its body sample and can correct XYZ before the stored floor is refreshed. The stored `roomFloor` is then measured from the corrected X/Z, resolving room membership at 381 above her origin before measuring the linked floor. It is not the previous tick's height, the front collision height, or necessarily the raw floor of her stored room. The initialization seed is an exception, described in the Great Wall start rules. |

**Established — retail II at `travel` tick 269.** At (64270, 32777) in X/Z, room 36 tile (62,32) has floor click 21 (5376), X slope 0 and Z slope +1. The Z offset 9 leaves an opposite-edge distance of 1014. Round the positive product 1014 divided by four down to 253; adding it gives 5629 for both Lara's Y and her stored floor.

**Established — clean-side comparison.** At the same sample point and sector, the current port instead makes the product negative before rounding, then subtracts the result. Dividing −1014 by four and rounding down gives −254; subtracting it gives 5630. The one-unit discrepancy is from that rounding order, not a different sample point or refresh time.

**Established — Tomb Raider I reference, separately.** The I wall reference uses the same world-axis interpretation, signed per-axis rounding, outer signs, and 1023 complement described here. This corrects the I trigger table's axis labels if read as world axes. The comparison is to the licensed reference for I, not a new check of the retail I executable or a claim that the port's I path matches it.

## Pose sampling at a clip's tail (Q-T2C-07)

**Established — which keys.** Count frames from the current animation's first authored frame. The preceding key's index is the number of whole keyframe spacings elapsed; the remainder is the number of ticks since that key. At remainder zero, use that key alone. At a nonzero remainder the far key is the **next physically stored key of the current clip's pose data**, even if its nominal frame is past the clip's authored end. It is not the first key of a successor clip or the destination of a state transition. Taking an animation link on a later tick is separate from sampling the current pose.

**Established — which divisor.** Ordinarily use the full authored keyframe spacing. The retail shortening test compares the next key's nominal frame **relative to the current clip's start** with the authored **absolute last-frame number**. Only if that relative number is strictly greater than the absolute last frame does the divisor shorten to the absolute last-frame number minus the preceding key's relative frame. Do not test both numbers in the same coordinate system: the retail comparison mixes the two. A physical tail shorter than the regular spacing therefore does not usually shorten the divisor, especially on a clip starting at a large absolute frame number. This supersedes the earlier description that the final interval always shrank to the clip's authored end.

**Established statically — pose and bounds.** Ordinary Lara body poses and ordinary animated item poses use the same stored pair and elapsed fraction for both translation and rotation, interpolating the pose transforms rather than replacing the next pose with the successor's. Interpolated bounds use that same pair and fraction. This is not a rule that all consumers interpolate: an exact key uses a single pose; a nearest-pose query can choose a single stored key instead. Separately selected weapon-arm poses and special hit poses are not evidence of a different clip-tail rule for the ordinary body.

| Checkpoint | Established derivation from the stored poses and camera rule |
|---|---|
| `roll` and `grab-attempt`, tick 163 | Current clip 147 spans 3903–3916 with spacing 3. Frame 3916 is 13 ticks from its start, one tick past key 4. Physically stored key 5 is the endpoint, not clip 148. The next key's relative frame 15 is not greater than the absolute end 3916, so the fraction is 1 of 3. Bounds (−519, 9) toward (−566, 5) give (−534, 8). With Lara Y 5451, the quarter-down aim is 5052; easing from 5114 by one quarter toward zero gives target Y **5099**. |

The aim is 4875; easing from 4942 gives target Y **4926**. The stand successor is not sampled as an animation link until the next tick. |

## Looking toward a sector's subject (Q-T2L-04)

| Situation | Established retail behaviour |
|---|---|
| First eligible tick | A selected automatic subject can turn Lara in the camera update of the same tick as the floor action, after Lara's movement and weapon update. A named subject for a fixed or heavy view instead directs that view toward the item; it does not give Lara this automatic turn. A cinematic view uses its separate camera. In `travel` and `jump` at tick 204 the look subject is the marker in **item slot 102** (object type 243), not the helicopter in slot 101. The sector activates slot 101 and names slot 102 for Look. |
| Desired yaw and pitch | Use the subject's X/Z origin and the midpoint of its current animated vertical bounds, truncating the midpoint division toward zero. At Lara use her current X/Z and the point one quarter down from the top of her animated bounds, rounding that quarter down. Both angles use the [integer-bearing convention for the start camera](tr2-lara-start.md#camera-seed-placement-and-the-opening-view). Wrap the bearing toward the item minus Lara's body yaw to a signed turn before halving it. For pitch, the forward component is horizontal distance (integer square root) and the signed upward component is Lara's aim height minus the subject's midpoint height. Halve both signed angles by rounding down. Accept only desired yaw strictly between −9100 and +9100 and desired pitch strictly between −15470 and +15470. Equality rejects; an out-of-range subject is not clamped. |
| Vertical bounds between keyframes | **Established.** Use the bounds of the current clip's stored keyframes, with the [tail rule above](#pose-sampling-at-a-clips-tail-q-t2c-07). Interpolate each bound using the signed difference, truncating toward zero before adding it to the preceding value. `jump` tick 205 does not distinguish this interpolation from holding the preceding keyframe: both give desired pitch 3777 and recorded pitch 1365. The two roll checkpoints above do distinguish the rule. |
| Applied head and torso | Each accepted axis approaches its desired half-angle by at most 728 per camera update, or the remaining difference if smaller. The torso receives the **same** pitch and yaw as the head, not a second independently smoothed value. The two together supply the turn; there is no roll from this action. Acceptance marks the subject as looked at and uses Look for that view. The first accepted tick in both routes sets head and torso to pitch +728 and yaw −728. This sharing and the automatic limits are inherited from I. |
| Renewal and loss | For ordinary auto-Look the camera remembers the subject as the previous item and clears the current selection after the view, returning to Follow for the next update. Continued tracking needs a new eligible nomination, so a subject already looked at can continue while it is the same as the previous item but is normally ignored on re-entry after losing it. A failed angle test does not mark a subject as looked at. See [camera renewal and competing records](tr2-triggers-and-interaction.md#item-masks-timers-and-action-ordering). In `travel`, 102 is the previous subject at ticks 204–247, but not at 248. |
| Ground decay | At the start of ordinary dry control, unless manual Look is accepted or the camera is already in Look mode, an old pitch or yaw component with magnitude below 364 snaps to zero. Otherwise it loses one eighth, with the eighth truncated toward zero; equality at 364 still decays. The torso takes the decayed values. Because auto-Look usually ended the previous tick back in Follow, this also happens **between** automatic turns toward a continuously nominated item. The turn from 728 to 1365 on the second subject tick demonstrates decay before the new turn; when the marker goes away the recovery continues without a new turn. `travel` yaw −356 on tick 268 becomes zero on tick 269. This dry decay is inherited from I. |
| Manual Look on dry ground | Eligible Left/Right and Forward/Back can influence the head instead of locomotion. See the [dry input rules](#manual-look-eligibility-on-dry-ground-q-t2c-06) below for the separate yaw permission and pitch states. Manual Look blocks new automatic nominations. |
| Weapons drawn | During ordinary play, drawing or ready firearms select Combat before floor actions, except when the view is already in Look or cinematic mode. Combat suppresses new automatic subject nominations. A paired gun's lock can also replace manual angles during the subsequent weapon update: one locked arm gives head and torso half that arm's aimed pitch and yaw, two locked arms give both one quarter of the sum (truncate division toward zero); neither locked does not replace those angles. A locked long gun gives torso the full aim and sets head pitch and yaw to zero. These are weapon aim rules separate from auto-Look; merely selecting a holstered gun is not Combat. |

## Manual Look eligibility on dry ground (Q-T2C-06)

**Established — yaw is permitted by the preceding tick.** With Look held and no scripted extra pose, Lara can use manual yaw if the previous dry control tick left Look permitted. A new playable start permits it. Ordinary dry control assumes that the next tick will permit it, except for the following current states, which disable yaw Look on the **next** tick:

| States that withdraw yaw Look permission for the next tick | State numbers |
|---|---|
| Dead or splat reaction | 8, 12 |
| Ground sidesteps | 21, 22 |
| Right or left airborne jump | 26, 27 |
| Pushing or pulling a block; collecting a pickup | 36, 37, 39 |
| Operating a switch in either direction, using a key or puzzle item | 40, 41, 42, 43 |

**Established — timing of the exclusion.** Dry states not listed above do not withdraw ordinary yaw Look permission. A tick that enters an excluded pose is not on that account already denied: the state must have withdrawn permission on a previous tick. Conversely, re-entering an eligible state does not override a denial already carried into that tick; ordinary input in the eligible state restores permission for the following tick. The exclusion does not consume Left or Right. In [`look-sidestep`](tr2-oracle-trace.md#observed-routes), the body steers during state 21 with Right and Look held at ticks 155–162, without changing head yaw. Lara ends tick 175 in Stand, but her last sidestep update has still withdrawn Look for 176. On 176 Right therefore starts state 6, restoring Look eligibility. On 177 Look consumes Right and adds +364 to head and torso yaw; the starting Turn Right state independently supplies its final +45 body turn while returning to Stand. From 178 the body stays still while the head and torso continue turning.

| Look action | Established effect on directions and pose |
|---|---|
| Yaw when permitted | Look selects the Look view even with no direction held. Left decrements head yaw by 364 and Right increments it by 364. Opposed Left and Right are already cancelled by normal input; if both were presented to the yaw rule, Left would win. A direction is consumed even when its bound prevents another step. The pre-step tests are strictly above −8008 to decrease and strictly below +8008 to increase. A step from an off-grid angle can pass the nominal bound; there is no final clamp. Use the same yaw for the torso unless Lara's hands are occupied or she's on a vehicle. I's wall reference uses the same rate and a wider ±9100 pre-step bound. |
| Pitch, a separate state rule | The ordinary dry states that accept pitch are **Stand (2), ordinary Hang (10), and ladder stance (56)**. Being permitted to use yaw does not itself grant pitch; Walk, Run, slide and other dry states do not consume Forward/Back for Look. A dry state can take pitch even if the previous state withheld yaw permission. Stand checks death and dry Roll first; in particular a Forward+Back combination that requests Roll can win before vertical Look. At the vertical Look opportunity, setting Look chooses the Look view. Forward decreases pitch and Back increases it by 364. Forward wins if both reach that opportunity. The pre-step bounds are strictly above −7644 to decrease or strictly below +4004 to increase, with no final clamp. Copy pitch to torso unless hands are occupied. |
| What still reaches movement | A direction consumed by Look is unavailable as a new movement request, even when its angle did not change at a bound. It does not cancel an existing motion or a body turn produced by the current state's own rate; see [sidestep and ordinary turn under Look](tr2-lara-ground-and-weapons.md#sidestep-and-standing-turn-q-t2c-10). In yaw-only states Forward/Back still reach movement. Refused Look leaves ordinary Left/Right available. Dedicated sidestep bits are separate from those ordinary directions and are not consumed by yaw Look. |
| Release, an automatic subject, and weapons | Releasing Look does not zero the angles immediately: the normal pitch/yaw recovery above resumes unless the camera is already in Look. At release in `look-sidestep`, yaw 5460 becomes 4778 on the next tick, then continues toward zero. A sector can nominate an eligible subject on a later tick, but release does not by itself renew a marker whose prior eligibility was lost. Accepted manual Look takes priority over automatic nomination and keeps the Look rig even with firearms ready. The separate firearm aiming update can still replace the head/torso turns if it locks on. Merely carrying drawn pistols is not a reason to refuse manual Look. |

**Established — paired-pistol holster and automatic gaze.** Without held Look, each paired-pistol holster tick replaces head and torso pitch and yaw, after the arm motion but before the camera can accept an automatic subject. For each axis, add both arms' current angles and divide by four, truncating toward zero; use that value on both head and torso. This also applies on the tick that finishes holstering and frees both hands. With Look held these angles are not replaced by holstering. In `look`, both arms have zero pitch and yaw throughout holstering, ticks 293–311 inclusive. Automatic subject 102 therefore turns anew from zero to +728 pitch and −728 yaw at each of those ticks, including 311 (which ends with free hands); this is not the normal release decay. On tick 312 holstering no longer replaces the angles and gaze continues from that first step.

**Established — playback and its limits.** `look` shows 364-unit yaw and pitch steps with no body movement in Stand. It enters marker 102's sector at tick 280 with ready pistols; the helicopter wakes without automatic gaze. Ticks 286–292 show manual yaw with pistols still ready. A later manual Look overrides a nominated marker: from pitch 3801, Back takes one full step to 4165 at tick 336, then no further step through tick 342, corroborating the positive pitch bound's pre-step test without a final clamp. `look-sidestep` shows refused Look with continued body turning, and the later decay upon release in Stand. The negative pitch and yaw limits, full exclusion list, hanging and ladder pitch, and the outcome with a live firearm lock are established statically, not exercised by these fixtures.
