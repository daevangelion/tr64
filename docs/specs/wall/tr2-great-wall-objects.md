Executable: `Tomb2.exe`, 912896 bytes; SHA-256 `fd9c91d14d54540b3e00c9bedafeef9e4ddb1e9542adc9f29170c7f19b1a657b`.
Inputs: retail executable and the authored PC level The Great Wall (`WALL.TR2`); I baseline on tr64 `cdfb71d`.

### Recorded support, crossing and collapse

**Established — wake is not the falling tick.** The floor evaluation can wake a slab after the item update. For a collapsing tile the next update tests Lara's exact feet height against the slab. On a mismatch it drops out of that update set without advancing the idle clip; the ordinary sector action can wake it again later that tick. The first update that **starts** in shaking state 1 requests state 2 before the pose advances. On `collapse-stand` tick 3, the goal is already 2 in clip 300/frame 2, while current state is still 1. Once the shake begins it completes with or without Lara on the tile. Entering state 2 removes support immediately; gravity begins on the first update that starts in 2 before requesting rest. Downward speed becomes 6 on that first update. Reaching the underlying floor snaps the tile there, clears fall speed and requests 3. Clip 302 then holds the settled pose. Its end deactivates the tile, but an untimed action still being evaluated can wake it in the same update, without restoring support or restarting the shake.

## Push/pull block

**Established — occupied and vacated support.** A resting block raises its sector 1024 and closes its blockable navigation box. The departure floor is restored and the box unblocked when the block starts moving; the arrival sector rises and blocks when the block settles, before its heavy evaluation. A one-shot removal restores the occupied cell. The Great Wall's item 30, type 67, uses states 1/2/3 in clips 309–311.

**Established — reconstruction.** In a room flip the outgoing cell loses the block's height change and the incoming one gains it. A load first restores the authored start cell; the saved cell is raised again for an inactive resting block, but not for a moving one. This comes from comparing the cell before and after occupancy, separately from which values are saved. **Inferred:** the ordinary skeleton-room save/load should not leave a second raised cell behind at the start; no round-trip was performed.

**Established — grasp and move.** Entry requires Action, free hands, a grounded stand at the block's height, and relative X −300…300, Y 0, Z −692…−512 with pitch/yaw/roll ±1820/±5460/±1820. Lara faces the nearest cardinal direction and stands 100 inside the near edge. Unlike I, II accepts a grasp with Forward or Back already held, and checks that the block's position belongs to its own room. Still in reach, the move can commit at frame 2091, 19 frames after animation 120 begins. Forward chooses push, Back alone chooses pull. Both held with a blocked push leaves Lara in readiness; it does not pull. Back alone can be tried on another eligible frame. The input contrast establishes priority; the block clips separately corroborate the 1024 translation and deactivation.

| Candidate move | What blocks it |
|---|---|
| Either direction | **Established, inherited.** Its own sector must be solid-wall height or raised by exactly 1024 over its base. The destination's base floor must be exactly at the block's original bottom, with no static geometry within the 500-radius, 1000-height movement footprint. This is a sector-height check as well as a scenery test; an empty-looking mesh alone is insufficient. |
| Push one block ahead | **Established, changed.** A sloped destination fails even if the raw floor height matches. Its sampled ceiling must be at or above a point 100 below the block's nominal top (the original bottom minus 1024); I checked clearance at the nominal top. The extra slope rejection and 100 allowance do not apply to the reverse pull test. |
| Pull one block toward herself | **Established, inherited.** Destination ceiling must be at or above the block's top. Lara also needs the same floor height one cell farther back and clearance at least 762 high, and no scenery overlap where she ends up, at radius 100 and height 762. Unlike the push, the pull compares the destination floor/cell and ceiling without the additional no-slope requirement. These are distinct outcomes for a blocking slope and for a ceiling or scenery obstruction. |

**Established — nearest-face push.** The contact tick places Lara on the nearest horizontal face of the item's current animation-frame bounds, each of the four edges extended by 100, and does not yet change her health. Take her X and Z minus the item's. Let s and c be the 14-bit sine and cosine of the item's yaw. Across is c times the X offset minus s times the Z offset, shifted down 14. Along is c times the Z offset plus s times the X offset, shifted down 14. A negative shift rounds toward more negative. The frame bound's X pair is across and its Z pair is along. If she is outside the extended box, leave her. Otherwise move her to the nearest extended face and invert the same products: new X is the item's X plus c times the new across plus s times the new along, shifted down 14; new Z is the item's Z plus c times the new along minus s times the new across, shifted down 14. When two faces are equally near, the earlier face in the door order wins: lower X, then higher X, then higher Z, then lower Z. An across face therefore beats an equally near along face, and the two across faces pick the lower one. That tie order is static; these records do not include an equal-distance case. Later ticks repeat the test against the item's new origin, so she keeps its step while the overlap lasts.
