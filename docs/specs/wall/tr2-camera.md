# Tomb Raider II: gameplay camera geometry and fixed-view renewal

Game and release: Tomb Raider II, Steam appid 225300, Windows PC `Tomb2.exe`, 912896 bytes, SHA-256 `fd9c91d14d54540b3e00c9bedafeef9e4ddb1e9542adc9f29170c7f19b1a657b`.
Reader: WallRE2. Date: 2026-11-22. Evidence: retail rules cross-checked against `WALL.TR2` sector, box, camera and pose data and the published `stand` and `travel` records. Scope: ordinary gameplay views, not cinematic playback. Y increases downward. One tile is 1024 units and one click is 256. The [startup specification](tr2-lara-start.md#camera-seed-placement-and-the-opening-view) records the first eye and the integer bearing convention. The [geometry specification](tr2-lara-slide-height-and-gaze.md) supplies slope heights, room selection and interpolated bounds.

The `stand` and `travel` checkpoints below exercise Follow, an untimed Fixed view without a fixed item subject, and automatically nominated Look. The `look` fixture additionally exercises manual Look and weapon-ready travel through a look-subject sector. Countdown expiry, Heavy, and changes to or from a fixed item subject are established statically, not by those checkpoints. The bounce rules are also static evidence; the hard-landing and fall-damage fixtures do not request a bounce.

## Aim point and modes (Q-T2C-03, Q-T2C-04)

| View | Established starting target and timing |
|---|---|
| Follow or Fixed without an item subject | Aim at Lara's X/Z and a point one quarter down from the top of her current interpolated vertical bounds, rounding the quarter down. If the last view also did not use a fixed item subject, ease target Y one quarter toward that aim, truncating toward zero. The target room starts at Lara's current room, then resolves at the target XYZ. An authored centre-follow request uses the midpoint of the subject's local front and back bounds (truncated division by two), projected along its yaw to move target X/Z before room resolution. |
| Fixed or Heavy with an item subject | Aim at the item's X/Z and the midpoint of its current vertical bounds, truncated toward zero. Use that item's room to start the target room check. With no change in whether the view uses a fixed item subject, the same quarter easing applies. On a change between using a fixed item subject and not using one, take the new target Y directly and use an eye divisor of 1. This is the change in subject kind, not a comparison of item numbers. |
| Automatic Look or manual Look | Automatic nomination first updates Lara's head and torso according to [the gaze rules](tr2-lara-slide-height-and-gaze.md#looking-toward-a-sectors-subject-q-t2l-04). It does not move the camera target toward the subject's origin. Manual Look supplies its own head/torso angles; an accepted automatic look and manual Look use the same camera rig. The aim height starts one click above the usual Lara aim point. If the preceding view did not use a fixed item subject, approach that height by one quarter, rounding **down** rather than toward zero. Use divisor 4 for the target's horizontal easing and the eye's movement. Leaving a fixed item subject instead takes that raised height directly and uses divisor 1. |
| Combat | Raise the aim one click and ease vertically like Look, but keep target X/Z exactly at Lara. Use divisor 8 or 1 on leaving a fixed item subject. The eye uses the direction, distance and blocked alternatives in [the Combat rig](#combat-view-q-t2c-13), not the manual Look placement. |

**Established — angle and product rounding.** Use 65536 units per turn. For sine, fold the angle into the first quadrant before rounding its magnitude down to a multiple of 16 turn units; the table value is sine at that point times 16384, rounded to nearest integer, with the quadrant's sign reapplied. Cosine is sine one quarter-turn ahead. Round signed trigonometric products down when dividing by 16384. For a desired distance, the horizontal radius is distance times cosine of elevation; the vertical offset is distance times sine of elevation. The eye's X/Z are behind the bearing by that horizontal radius (subtract the rounded sine product for X, cosine product for Z). These integer rules explain the −1085 vertical offset of the Great Wall slide view rather than an ideal real-valued 45° displacement.

### Exact signed bearing

**Established.** For a horizontal direction use X and Z differences from the eye to its target (for pitch, the signed vertical and unsigned horizontal components take their respective places). A zero vector gives zero. The table index is the smaller absolute component times 2048 divided by the larger, discarding the remainder. The table angle T is the arctangent of the index divided by 2048, as a fraction of a turn scaled to 65536 and rounded to nearest. The octant uses “X larger” only when its magnitude is strictly larger; equal components use the Z-larger-or-equal column. Zero counts as nonnegative. Wrap the result to −32768…32767 after the indicated addition, subtraction or complement.

| Z sign | X sign | Z larger or equal | X larger |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonnegative | Nonnegative | T | 16384 minus T |
| Nonnegative | Negative | 65535 minus T, which wraps to −1 minus T | T minus 16384 |
| Negative | Nonnegative | 32768 minus T | 16384 plus T |
| Negative | Negative | T minus 32768 | −16384 minus T |

The positive-Z, negative-X octant with Z larger or equal uses a complement against **65535**, not 65536. At `travel` tick 205 the eye-to-target direction is X −8, Z +1218; the table index is 13 and T is 66, giving signed bearing −67 rather than −66. The startup view is in the other positive-Z, negative-X octant.

**Established — the Look rig.** Its yaw is Lara's body yaw plus head yaw plus torso yaw, wrapped to a turn. Its elevation is body pitch plus head and torso pitch, also wrapped. The distance is 1536. Start from Lara's X/Z at the eased aim height. Offset horizontally along **body yaw** by minus 512 times the elevation sine, with that signed product rounded down at the trig scale. If the displaced point is outside floor-to-ceiling space, restore Lara's X/Z. Then apply the point clearance described below with margin 306 on X, Z and Y. This resolves the target room at the **unblended** point, before horizontal easing. Form the nominal eye behind this unblended target from the Look bearing and elevation. Use the shared blocked-eye procedure below, with ray shortening at the sides rather than radius recovery. Then ease only target X and Z from their previous values, each by the signed difference divided by the view divisor, truncating toward zero. Keep the target's clamped Y and the resolved room; do not re-resolve the blended X/Z. Move the eye after this horizontal target easing. Divisor 1 on leaving a fixed item subject applies to **both** horizontal target easing and eye movement, so neither retains a fraction of its old position.

## Two different ways to keep a point clear (Q-T2C-01, Q-T2C-02)

**Established — a sight line is not a navigation-box test.** Trace from target to destination against the live linked floor and ceiling with **no margin**. Both equality cases are clear. Sweep crossing planes on the horizontal axis with the smaller absolute displacement first (Z first on a tie), then on the other axis, from the same target but toward the possibly shortened endpoint. A zero displacement needs no sweep. Each sweep, beginning with the target's room, checks both sides of each crossed tile boundary in travel order: for decreasing coordinates the boundary itself and the unit just below it; for increasing coordinates the unit just below and then the boundary itself. Only test a crossing when the endpoint lies strictly beyond its near sample. At successive clear samples use the room selected for the preceding sample. The two sides have identical height and transverse coordinate. At the first impermissible point, retain the near sample as the endpoint; that sample's room is used even if the adjacent far side failed.

**Established — line arithmetic and final plane.** The transverse and Y rate for an axis sweep are their respective differences multiplied by 1024 and divided by the signed axis difference, truncating toward zero. At the first crossing multiply each rate by the signed distance along that axis from target to crossing and divide by 1024, rounding down; successive 1024-unit crossings add or subtract the rate according to direction. If the last sweep does not fail specifically on a far-side sample, resolve the endpoint and also test whether the endpoint floor or ceiling lies strictly between target Y and endpoint Y in the relevant direction. Where it does, shorten to that Y and rescale X/Z on the segment using signed division toward zero. Any blocked sweep or such endpoint shortening makes the sight line fail, even if the returned shortened endpoint is usable.

**Established — steep versus full slope height.** Normally the camera's pre-movement tests flatten a floor or ceiling tilt if either coefficient has magnitude 3 or more. A request for full slopes (for example forward slide) uses the real tilt. In Follow and Fixed, if the target is lower than the measured floor, the remaining tests also use full slopes. The common eye-movement step always uses full slopes. Thus the exact same ray can succeed when full tilt is considered and fail with flattened tilt.

**Established — point clearance.** This is a different operation from tracing a line. Resolve the point at its original XYZ and take that sector's navigation box. Each box has inclusive low and high world limits: the high edge is one less than the next tile boundary. Consider Z first then X. If the point is outside the box inset by the requested margin on a side, test a point one margin farther out in that direction, at the original other coordinates and Y. If that test is outside the floor-to-ceiling interval, bring the point to the box edge inset by the margin. Otherwise leave that axis alone. Check the low side first, and the high side if the low side made no correction. Determine vertical clearance from the floor and ceiling at the **original** point, not at the corrected X/Z: floor minus the margin and ceiling plus it, replacing an inverted interval with its midpoint rounded down. The vertical adjustment is applied for the Look target. When this clearance corrects a shortened fixed-eye destination it changes X/Z and retains the ray's Y; vertical clearance of the moved eye is handled separately.

**Established statically — point without a box.** The stock point correction has no special no-box branch: it uses the sector's no-box value as if it named a box, although there is no authored rectangle for that value. It does not reject the point or guarantee unchanged X/Z. Its vertical calculation still follows from the floor and ceiling at the original point if the correction completes; it does not require a valid box height. The horizontal outcome with no authored box was not verified in play and is not a geometry rule to infer from the valid-box cases.

## Fixed eye and Follow eye (Q-T2C-01, Q-T2C-02)

**Established — Fixed.** Begin each update of the fixed view with its authored eye XYZ and room, not the previous corrected destination. Test sight from the current target to that eye. On failure, apply the point clearance with margin 256 to the shortened endpoint, keeping the ray's Y. Use that result as the desired eye for this update. Box margins are used on a failed line in this mode; they are not the initial occlusion test. An untimed fixed view can be corrected without either ending or changing the selected view. Its next update starts with the authored coordinates again.

**Established — Follow.** Use Lara's body yaw plus the requested relative yaw. Add body pitch to the requested elevation and limit the result to −15470 through +15470. The ordinary requests are zero, with distance 1536. [The request belongs to the state that began Lara's update](#state-requests-before-dry-motion-q-t2c-08); the view then uses her updated position and orientation. The follow eye uses the horizontal radius and projected nominal XYZ above. Apply the following blocked-eye procedure, retaining an eye that needs no side correction, then move from the previous eye, not from the target or authored spawn camera.

### Shared blocked-eye procedure for Follow and Look

**Established — box and four samples.** First shorten the nominal goal by the target-to-goal sight line, even if its result is blocked. Begin with the box at the target's X/Z in its current room. If the resulting endpoint is outside that box and its own sector has a box, use the endpoint's box instead. At the endpoint's Y and other coordinate, probe the first unit beyond the current tile on each of the four horizontal sides, starting each probe from the **shortened endpoint's resolved room**, not the target room. Thus at a low edge the probe is boundary minus one, and at a high edge it is the next tile's first coordinate. A blocked probe supplies **its own coordinate** as the boundary. A clear probe with a box can extend the corresponding box bound outward; a clear probe without a box leaves that bound unchanged. Inset all four bounds by 256 (add on low sides, subtract on high sides). A side needs correction only when the endpoint is strictly beyond its inset bound and the probe on that side was blocked. First consider the axis on which the endpoint and target are farther apart, X winning a tie. On each axis consider its low side before its high side. Use the first side that qualifies; if none qualify, accept this shortened endpoint.

**Established statically — neither point has a box.** The shared procedure still attempts the target's box-bound calculation, with no authored bounds for that no-box value. If the endpoint also has no box, it cannot replace those bounds with a valid rectangle. Retail has no explicit branch to keep that endpoint or refuse it solely for lacking a box. No definite eye correction is established for this unsupported case; the point's sight-line result is still defined by the terrain test.

**Established — two candidates, with one sight test.** Both start from the same shortened endpoint. One uses the low perpendicular bound as its chosen side and the other the high perpendicular bound. Below, call the breached inset bound the near edge and the other bound on that axis the opposite edge. If correction is on the **first** axis considered, prefer low when the previous eye's perpendicular coordinate is strictly less than the target's; otherwise prefer high. If correction falls back to the **second** axis, make that same comparison with the **shortened endpoint**, not the previous eye. The preferred candidate is the only one sight-tested: use it if clear; on failure use the other candidate as calculated, without a second test or inheriting the failed one's shortening. Resolve the chosen XYZ starting from the shortened endpoint's room before side correction, not the target room or a candidate's own sight-test room. These are alternatives around one obstruction, not a search of the room graph.

**Established — Follow candidate on a blocked side.** Let A be the corner at the near edge and chosen side; B the near edge and other side; C the opposite edge and chosen side. Compare their **horizontal squared** distances from the target to the squared horizontal radius. For each candidate, use the first applicable row; its Y stays at the shortened endpoint's Y.

| Condition, in priority order | Established resulting horizontal position |
|---|---|
| A beyond the radius | Keep the near edge coordinate; recover the remaining radius toward the chosen side. The other offset from the target is the integer square root of radius squared less the squared offset to that edge. If the difference is negative, leave the other coordinate alone. |
| Otherwise, A's squared distance is strictly greater than 116281 (341 squared) | Use A. Do not round a square root before comparing; equality does not select this row. |
| Otherwise, B beyond the radius | Keep the near edge and recover the remaining radius toward the other side, with the same square-root and negative case. |
| Otherwise, C's distance squared exceeds twice the radius squared | Keep the chosen side and recover along the primary axis toward the opposite edge, using twice the radius squared less the chosen side's squared offset. If that difference is negative, keep the original endpoint in both axes. |
| Otherwise | Use C if it is at least as far from the target as B; use B if B is farther. |

**Established — Look candidate on a blocked side.** It stays on the line from the *unblended* Look target to the shortened eye. Clip at the near edge if the target is on its interior side (equality counts at a low edge, not at a high edge); recompute the perpendicular coordinate and Y at the intersection. If the resulting segment straddles the candidate's chosen side, with target strictly inside and eye strictly outside, clip again at that perpendicular boundary and recompute the remaining coordinates. For either intersection, multiply the endpoint-to-target difference on each other axis by the elapsed distance along the clipped axis, then divide by the total signed distance on that axis, truncating toward zero. Unlike Follow, it can change the goal's Y and does not restore radius with a square root.

### Move the eye and store the view

**Established.** Start with the previous raw eye. For each axis add the difference to the chosen goal divided by the actual movement divisor, truncating toward zero. Use the goal's room as the starting room for the new eye check. At the moved XYZ measure the floor with full slopes. If **both** moved Y and goal Y are at or below that floor minus 256, trace once more from the now-current target to the moved eye and retain the returned point. **Established statically:** the retry sweeps seed from the target's room; the endpoint is initially the moved eye in the room just resolved for it. The sweeps can replace that endpoint room. Resolve the returned point and measure the floor again, starting from the room the retry returned. The sampled cases do not distinguish the sweep's source room from the endpoint's seed. The final ceiling is measured with full slopes too. The clearance band is ceiling +256 through floor −256, or their midpoint rounded down if they overlap. The raw eye Y is retained separately from the vertical correction into that band; the corrected height is used for viewing and final eye-room resolution. This is why a trace's raw eye coordinates need not themselves respect the display margin. The recorded yaw is the [exact signed bearing](#exact-signed-bearing) from the moved eye toward the final target in ordinary play. A component whose divided change is zero can stay still indefinitely; there is no minimum one-unit step, or stall counter that eventually snaps the eye to a goal in these modes. The clearance/line checks still occur on that tick.

**Established — used divisor versus stored `speed`.** An authored fixed camera request uses one plus eight times its speed code; the initial view 2 has code zero, so its first move uses 1. A continuing untimed fixed view renews the camera number without rearming the speed; it can then use 10 carried from the preceding tick. Follow ordinarily uses the carried value 10 (1 at initial setup); Look and Combat ordinarily use 4 and 8. A change between aiming at Lara and at a fixed or heavy item subject uses divisor 1 and adopts the new aim height immediately rather than easing the old height. This includes returning to Follow from an item view. Changing between two item subjects is not by itself this change of category. After an ordinary Fixed, Look, Combat or Follow update, the next view starts from Follow defaults: `speed` 10, current index and current subject unset, zero yaw and elevation requests, distance 1536. The previous index and subject retain the just-used selections and `lastFixed` says whether it aimed at a fixed or heavy item subject. A continuing heavy view does not reset to Follow defaults until expiry. This accounts for `stand` tick 1 recording `speed` 10 after a divisor-1 move, with `lastIndex` 2 and the sampled mode already Follow. The placed view of boulder 73 also switches without easing at entry, early exit, re-entry and expiry; it does not activate the boulder.

**Established — what a countdown means off the trigger.** A fixed or heavy view moves the eye before decrementing its countdown. Zero means untimed; the last fixed update converts 1 to −1, not zero. When a sector stops requesting a fixed view, the eye can return to Follow even if the stored timer remains positive. With no trigger to evaluate, Follow leaves that value unchanged. If an evaluated trigger leaves no current camera selected at renewal, the remaining positive countdown becomes −1. This qualifies “with no current camera number” in the [trigger renewal rule](tr2-triggers-and-interaction.md#item-masks-timers-and-action-ordering): positive expiry there requires an evaluated trigger record. The previous view number can also remain visible for an expired view while Lara is still on its sectors. Neither the timer nor the previous index alone proves that a fixed eye was used.

**Established — comparison with the placed records.** In `fixed-camera-repeat`, view 4 still aims at boulder 73 at tick 57 with timer 49. Follow starts on tick 58, and clears both the previous view and item while the countdown remains 49. This value persists until the separate music-only trigger at 92, when it becomes −1. View 4 re-arms at 222. At 311 the timer becomes −1 while it still aims at the boulder; at 312 it returns to Follow and loses the subject. In `fixed-camera` the one-shot view 1 likewise still moves as fixed on the tick the timer becomes −1, but cannot re-arm after Lara leaves and returns.

### Bounce and shake (Q-T2C-05)

**Established statically — order.** The common eye move first eases toward the goal, resolves the moved eye, and computes the vertical clearance band, including any low-eye sight-line correction. It applies the bounce offsets to raw eye and target coordinates **after** measuring that band and **before** selecting the vertical display correction. After the offsets it uses the previously measured band; it does not measure a new band at the shaken X/Z. The eye's final room is resolved at its displaced X/Z and display-corrected Y. The target's offsets do not independently refresh its room. The view and ordinary eye-to-target yaw use the displaced points.

**Established — footfalls (animation effect 1), inherited from I.** Measure the signed X, Y and Z differences from the previous raw camera eye to the animated item's origin at the cue, before its ordinary stride. This is the eye left by the preceding camera update, not the current goal, target or sound listener. If any absolute difference is 16384 or more, leave the existing bounce alone. Otherwise sum the three squared differences and divide by 256 rounding down; subtract that result from 1048576, multiply by 100, then divide by 1048576 truncating toward zero. This signed value replaces any pending bounce, including when zero or negative; it is not added to the old value. The cue itself draws no randomness. The common eye move below consumes the resulting bounce, with its usual random offsets if it is negative.

**Established — sampling discriminator.** At `rex-mask` tick 491 the preceding raw eye is (67984, 25281, 74651) and the Tyrannosaurus starts at (80561, 26707, 74237). This gives a positive kick of 40, added once to both eye Y and target Y. Using its moved origin (80454, 26734, 74237) instead would give 41. The pre-stride ordering is also corroborated by the authored cue taking effect before the animation's ordinary displacement.

| Bounce value entering the move | Established effect |
|---|---|
| Zero | No offset and no random draw from bounce. |
| Positive | Add that value to eye Y and target Y once, leaving X/Z alone. Clear the value to zero. No random draw. |
| Negative | Draw **three successive values from the gameplay stream**, the first `rng` entry in the trace, not the presentation stream. Each draw yields a number 0–32767. Subtract 16384, multiply by the signed bounce value, then divide by 32767, truncating toward zero. All three offsets use the same original bounce value. |

| Negative-bounce draw | Eye offset | Target offset |
|---|---|---|
| First | Add to X. | Add to **Y**, not X. |
| Second | Add to Y. | Add to Y. |
| Third | Add to Z. | Add to Z. |

**Established statically — decay and comparison.** After the three draws the bounce value increases by five, without a separate clamp at zero. Thus a negative bounce can make both of its first two draws affect target height, while target X stays unchanged. This is consistent with the first game's wall reference's horizontal kick to target Y, not an equal offset on every corresponding axis. The same retail II stream supplies live shot randomness; the separate presentation stream is not touched by this bounce. **Playback limit:** `hard-landing` and `fall-damage` show clip 24 without bounce; each retains a constant gameplay RNG state. They prove that those impacts do not exercise a shake, not that a real shake leaves gameplay RNG unchanged.

## Combat view (Q-T2C-13)

**Established — entry and exit.** An admitted gun draw requests Combat on its first animation update, including when the arms are still empty: weapon status 2 (drawing) or 4 (ready). The update completing a draw still uses that draw's request. Free hands, hands busy with an interaction, a flare draw and gun holster (status 3) do not request Combat. A new holster takes effect before the weapon's view choice, so the **first** holster tick can already use Follow or Look. Releasing Action does not end Combat while the guns stay ready.

| Competing request | Established result |
|---|---|
| Accepted held Look | Manual Look wins; a drawing or ready gun does not replace it. A cinematic view already in force is likewise not replaced by the weapon. |

If the renewal sees a matching prior camera index while Combat or Look is active, it expires the retained timer to −1. ## Camera action grammar and renewal (load-time question)

**Established.** Action words are 16-bit. The low ten bits identify the action's operand and bits 10–13 give the action kind. Bit 15 is the end mark; it has two distinct interpretations for a camera action. Kind 1 has a second 16-bit word. Its low eight bits give seconds (zero for untimed), bit 8 is the one-shot request, and bits 9–13 are the speed code. Actual trigger execution and object-surface evaluation consume both words and take the end mark **only from the extra word**. In the fixed-view renewal reading, a camera action still consumes both words, but takes the end mark **only from the action word**, ignoring the mark in the extra word. Non-camera actions consume one word and use its own end mark. A switch, key or pickup controlling-item word precedes the true actions; execution handles it as an owner, whereas renewal reads it in sequence as an ordinary action word. A heavy view already in force skips renewal.

**Established — verify both extents, not one.** A real execution terminator on a camera extra word does not imply that renewal ends there. For example the Great Wall's opening fixed views end execution at the extra word, and renewal continues to the next authored word. The level check needs to account for the two readings separately. A matching previous camera index can renew an unexpired view unless Look or Combat is in force; a different index cannot renew it simply by being present. The separate [trigger specification](tr2-triggers-and-interaction.md#item-masks-timers-and-action-ordering) describes the ownership, competing subjects, expired timer, and rearming cases. The two termination rules do not make arbitrary reads beyond valid level data acceptable.
